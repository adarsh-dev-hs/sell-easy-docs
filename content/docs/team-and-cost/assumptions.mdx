Every number in this tab rests on the assumptions below. They are numbered so they can be cited in review. When one changes, change it here first, then follow [How to update the model](#how-to-update-the-model).

<Callout type="warn" title="The five to challenge first">
**A1** — three months is enough. **P1** — CTC is the fully-loaded cost. **AI1** — AI assistance gives a 1.25–1.35× throughput multiplier. **V1** — pilot customers bring their own vendor keys. **G2** — ₹88 per USD. If any of these is wrong, the budget is wrong by a lot, not a little.
</Callout>

## General

| # | Assumption | Value used | If it is wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G1** | Currency | All figures in **Indian rupees (₹)**, Indian digit grouping, lakh (L) and crore (Cr) for large sums | — |
| **G2** | Exchange rate | **₹88 = $1** for every cloud, AI and vendor price | ±5% moves the non-people budget by about ∓₹40,000 |
| **G3** | Start date | Monday **5 October 2026** | A late start compresses the [capacity model](/docs/team-and-cost/team-plan#capacity-model), it does not extend the deadline |
| **G4** | Duration | **3 months**, ending **31 December 2026** — 13 calendar weeks, week 13 partial | Every extra fortnight costs ≈ ₹2.2L |
| **G5** | Month length | Calendar months. Month 1 is charged as a full month even though work starts on the 5th | Pro-rating month 1 saves ≈ ₹49,000 |
| **G6** | Costs are gross | No revenue, grants, credits (AWS Activate) or tax effects netted off | Credits could remove the entire ₹70,000 infrastructure line |
| **G7** | Inflation and raises | None inside the three months | — |
| **G8** | Contingency | **10–15%**; **12.5%** used in all summaries | — |
| **G9** | Scope | As in [Roadmap](/docs/roadmap) and [MVP scope](/docs/mvp-scope) | Scope growth shows up as schedule slip, which is the most expensive failure mode |
| **G10** | Excluded entirely | Founder salary or draw, office, marketing, sales commissions, legal entity setup, penetration testing, SOC 2, notice-period buyouts, hardware beyond laptops and peripherals | These are real costs; budget them separately |

## People

| # | Assumption | Value used | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | **CTC is the fully-loaded employer cost** | No multiplier applied on top of CTC | This is what CTC means in India. It covers base, HRA, allowances, **employer PF**, **gratuity accrual**, **bonus/variable**, and **insurance premiums** |
| **P2** | What CTC excludes | Laptops, software and AI seats, recruitment fees, workspace, offsites, ESOPs | Budgeted separately as "Tooling & one-offs" — ₹5,50,000 expected |
| **P3** | Team composition | **Exactly 3 engineers**: 1 SSE (tech lead) + 2 SE. No PM, designer, DevOps or QA | Founder acts as PM; SSE owns infrastructure; the team tests its own work. Risks in [Team plan](/docs/team-and-cost/team-plan#what-this-team-does-not-cover) |
| **P4** | Salaries — expected | SSE **₹22,00,000** CTC; SE **₹12,00,000** CTC each. Combined **₹46,00,000** = **₹3,83,333 / month** | 3-month people cost **₹11,50,000** |
| **P5** | Salaries — bands | Low: ₹19L + 2 × ₹10L = ₹39L. High: ₹26L + 2 × ₹15L = ₹56L | Reflects Bengaluru / Pune / Hyderabad / NCR / Chennai product-company ranges |
| **P6** | Location | All three in India, one time zone, remote-first with no office | Removes co-working from the expected case; it appears in the high case at ₹81,000 |
| **P7** | Employer PF | Contributed on the **₹15,000 wage ceiling** (~₹1,800/month), inside CTC | PF on full basic would raise employer cost materially — **verify with payroll** |
| **P8** | Variable pay | The **full CTC is assumed spent** inside the window | If bonus (~12% of CTC) is genuinely conditional and deferred, in-window cash falls by ≈ ₹1,38,000 |
| **P9** | Hiring on time | All three start on 5 October; no notice-period gaps | A three-week late start saves ₹69,000 and costs 3 of 27 engineer-weeks |
| **P10** | Attrition | **None** inside the three months | A resignation in a 13-week plan is close to unrecoverable; there is no bench |
| **P11** | Laptops | 3 new machines at **₹1,10,000** each = ₹3,30,000, expensed in month 1 | Alternatives: leasing ≈ ₹45,000, refurbished ≈ ₹2L, own machines ₹0 |
| **P12** | Recruitment | **₹1,50,000** expected — partly referral, partly paid sourcing | High case assumes an agency at 8.33% of first-year CTC on all three (₹3,83,000) |
| **P13** | Equity | Excluded — ESOPs are non-cash and outside CTC | |
| **P14** | Contractors | Not in the expected plan. Indicative rates given as a fallback | Indian contractors run **1.5×–3×** an equivalent FTE monthly; see [contractor alternative](/docs/team-and-cost/people-cost#contractor-alternative) |

## AI-assisted development

This is a distinct assumption class because the plan depends on it.

| # | Assumption | Value used | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AI1** | Throughput multiplier | **1.25–1.35× overall.** 1.4–1.8× on mechanical work (~50% of scope), 1.2–1.4× on semi-novel (~30%), 1.0–1.1× on genuinely hard work (~20%) | 27 net engineer-weeks behave like ~34–36 conventional engineer-weeks. **This is the assumption that makes 3 engineers in 3 months feasible** |
| **AI2** | Tooling per engineer | **One paid Claude Code seat each** (Max 5× tier, ~$100 ≈ ₹8,800/month) plus ~₹4,400/month of shared API usage | ₹30,800/month for the team; ₹92,000 over three months |
| **AI3** | Review overhead | **Increases**, not decreases, with AI assistance. The 15% non-feature overhead in the capacity model is set higher than a non-AI team's for this reason | Every PR has a named human owner who can explain it |
| **AI4** | What AI does not accelerate | Vendor approvals, DNS and domain warm-up, security reviews, production debugging, RDS restores — all take the same wall-clock time | |
| **AI5** | Capacity | 39 gross engineer-weeks − 1.5 onboarding − 3.0 holidays − 2.0 leave − 1.0 year-end = 31.5 net, − 15% overhead = **≈ 27 engineer-weeks** of feature work | ~9 engineer-weeks per roadmap month |
| **AI6** | Holidays | ~4–5 Indian public holidays in Oct–Dec including the Dussehra–Diwali cluster and Christmas | **Confirm against the 2026 calendar for your state** — holiday lists vary |

## Infrastructure

| # | Assumption | Value used | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I1** | Cloud and region | **AWS us-east-1**, on-demand list prices, no Savings Plans, no Reserved Instances | A three-month window is too short to commit to either |
| **I2** | No credits | **AWS Activate and free-tier benefits excluded** from the estimate | Activate gives $1,000–$100,000; **$1,000 ≈ ₹88,000**, more than the entire infra line. Apply in week 1 |
| **I3** | Architecture | [Target architecture](/docs/architecture/target-architecture), introduced progressively | ECS Fargate (or App Runner for the lean option), RDS Postgres, ALB, S3 + CloudFront or Vercel, Secrets Manager, KMS, CloudWatch, SES |
| **I4** | Scale | Pilot: ≤ 10 workspaces, ≤ 50 users, ~20k signals and ~10k emails per month, a few GB of data | |
| **I5** | Not provisioned | **No ElastiCache Redis, no OpenSearch, no Redshift** in these three months | Postgres `pg_trgm` covers search at this scale. Redis would add ~$12/month if needed |
| **I6** | RDS configuration | **Single-AZ** throughout the build | No automatic failover. Revisit before real customer data volume grows |
| **I7** | Environments | Dev + staging + production by December; lean option collapses to one environment | Stakeholder demo runs in mock mode with no backend, so it is effectively free |
| **I8** | Frontend hosting | **Vercel Pro**, 2 members (~₹3,520–₹5,280/month), for preview deployments | CloudFront + S3 is cheaper (~₹700–₹1,760) but costs engineer time the plan does not have |
| **I9** | Ramp | ₹10,500 (M1) → ₹24,500 (M2) → ₹35,000 (M3) = **₹70,000**; low ₹30,000, high ₹1,26,000 (×1.8) | |
| **I10** | Excluded | AWS support plans, GST, Marketplace purchases | |

## Vendors, AI usage and tooling

| # | Assumption | Value used | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **V1** | **Credential model** | **Pilot customers bring their own vendor keys.** SellEasy pays only for development and sandbox tiers | If SellEasy must supply data instead, this line goes from ₹75,000 to **₹2L–₹5L+** in the quarter |
| **V2** | Vendor prices | Indicative public entry tiers. Quote-based vendors **must be verified** | Clearbit/Breeze, Apollo, RB2B, Trigify, Instantly, Mailpool, HeyReach |
| **V3** | Bombora | **Not in the expected or high case** | An enterprise intent data agreement runs to tens of lakhs a year. If signed, redo the budget |
| **V4** | Vendor ramp | ₹13,900 (M1) → ₹27,300 (M2) → ₹33,800 (M3) = **₹75,000**; low ₹30,000, high ₹1,80,000 | |
| **A1** | **LLM prices** | Opus 5 **$5 / $25**, Sonnet 5 **$2 / $10**, Haiku 4.5 **$1 / $5** per million input / output tokens. At ₹88: Opus ₹440 / ₹2,200; Sonnet ₹176 / ₹880; Haiku ₹88 / ₹440 | **Assumption — verify current Anthropic pricing** |
| **A2** | Cached reads and batch | Cached input ≈ 10% of the input price; Batch API ≈ 50% of token prices | Not applied in the headline scenarios — treat as upside |
| **A3** | Tokens per task | Outreach draft **3,000 in / 800 out**; reply triage **1,500 / 200**; account research **20,000 / 2,000** | Gives ₹3.08, ₹1.23 and ₹0.62 per draft on Opus, Sonnet and Haiku |
| **A4** | Regeneration factor | **1.2** — one draft in five is regenerated once | |
| **A5** | Product LLM volumes | Dev/eval in M1–M2; **200 drafts + 20 replies per day** across 3–5 pilot workspaces in M3 | ₹6,000 → ₹9,000 → ₹23,000 = **₹38,000**; low ₹15,000, high ₹75,000 |
| **A6** | Default model | **Opus 5** for pilot drafting | Switching drafts to Sonnet saves ≈ ₹13,000 per pilot month. Run an eval set before deciding |
| **T1** | Team tooling | GitHub, Sentry, Linear, Figma, Google Workspace, warm-up mailboxes, password manager ≈ **₹11,650/month** + ₹5,000 of domains | **₹40,000** over three months; low ₹18,000, high ₹75,000 |
| **T2** | Observability | CloudWatch only (inside the infrastructure line) plus Sentry. **No Datadog** | |

## How to update the model

<Steps>
<Step>
### Change the assumption here first

Edit the value in the tables above. Every downstream number cites one of these.
</Step>
<Step>
### Recompute people cost

Monthly run rate = Σ (CTC ÷ 12) per role. Multiply by 3. Do it for the low, expected and high bands. Update [People cost](/docs/team-and-cost/people-cost#the-run-rate).

```text
Current: (₹22,00,000 ÷ 12) + 2 × (₹12,00,000 ÷ 12)
       = ₹1,83,333 + ₹2,00,000
       = ₹3,83,333 / month  ×3 = ₹11,50,000
```
</Step>
<Step>
### Recompute the one-off bucket

Laptops + peripherals + recruitment + workspace + 3 months of tooling. All of it except tooling lands in month 1.
</Step>
<Step>
### Recompute the non-people ramps

Infrastructure, vendors, AI dev tooling and product LLM usage each have a three-row monthly ramp. Convert any USD price at the **G2** rate. Apply the low and high multipliers stated on each page.
</Step>
<Step>
### Add up and apply contingency

People + tooling & one-offs + vendors & AI + infrastructure, then × 1.125 (or 1.10 / 1.15). Update [Total budget](/docs/team-and-cost/total-budget).
</Step>
<Step>
### Update the pages in this order

[People cost](/docs/team-and-cost/people-cost) → [Infrastructure cost](/docs/team-and-cost/infrastructure-cost) → [Vendor & AI cost](/docs/team-and-cost/vendor-and-ai-cost) → [Total budget](/docs/team-and-cost/total-budget) → [overview](/docs/team-and-cost). Check that the headline figure on the overview matches.
</Step>
</Steps>

A spreadsheet with one sheet per page (salaries, one-offs, ramps, totals) is the practical way to keep these consistent; the tables in this tab are its published view.
