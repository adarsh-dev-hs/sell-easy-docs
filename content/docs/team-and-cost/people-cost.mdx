The arithmetic, spelled out:

```text
SSE   ₹22,00,000 ÷ 12                 = ₹1,83,333 / month
SE    ₹12,00,000 ÷ 12 = ₹1,00,000 × 2 = ₹2,00,000 / month
                                 ----------------------------
Run rate                              = ₹3,83,333 / month
× 3 months                            = ₹11,50,000  (₹11.5L)
Annualised run rate                   = ₹46,00,000  (₹46L)
```

<Callout type="info" title="Month 1 is a full month">
The team starts Monday **5 October 2026**, so October is about 27 calendar days of employment rather than 31. This model charges a **full month** anyway: salaries are monthly, not daily, and most offers start on the 1st or are paid in full for the joining month. If your offers are pro-rated from the 5th, month 1 people cost falls by roughly **₹49,000**.
</Callout>

## What CTC covers

In India, **CTC (cost to company) is the fully-loaded employer cost of employing someone**, which is exactly what a budget needs. That is why this model uses CTC directly with no additional multiplier.

<Callout type="info" title="Inside a typical ₹22L or ₹12L CTC">
**Usually included**

- Basic salary, HRA and special allowance
- **Employer's PF contribution** — 12% of basic, commonly restricted to the ₹15,000 wage ceiling (about ₹1,800 per month, ₹21,600 a year)
- **Gratuity accrual** — about 4.81% of basic, set aside against the statutory payment at five years' service
- **Performance bonus or variable pay** — often 10–15% of CTC, paid quarterly or annually and payable only on performance
- **Group health and term insurance premiums**
- Sometimes a flexible-benefits wallet (LTA, telephone, fuel, meal cards)

**Usually not included**

- Laptops and hardware
- Software licences and AI seats
- Recruitment or agency fees
- Workspace or co-working desks
- Team offsites, professional development budgets
- ESOPs (non-cash, and separate from CTC)
</Callout>

**Two things to check with your payroll provider before locking the budget:**

1. **Is the variable component inside CTC?** If bonus is 12% of CTC and is genuinely conditional, your expected cash outflow in a three-month window is closer to **₹3,37,000 per month** than ₹3,83,333 — around **₹1.4L less** across the three months. This model conservatively assumes the full CTC is spent.
2. **Is PF on the full basic or on the ₹15,000 ceiling?** Contributing on full basic raises employer PF materially at these salary levels. If your CTC letters were built on the ceiling but payroll runs on full basic, the difference lands on you, not on the employee.

**Take-home, for offer conversations only.** Under the new tax regime and with PF on the ceiling, ₹22L CTC is roughly **₹1.55L per month in hand** and ₹12L CTC is roughly **₹90,000**. Indicative only — it depends on regime choice, declarations and salary structure, and it does not change the budget. Confirm with a payroll provider.

<Callout type="warn" title="Laptops are the biggest single discretionary line after salary">
₹3.3L of hardware against ₹11.5L of salary looks disproportionate because the window is three months. The options, cheapest first: engineers use their own machines (₹0, but a security and support headache), lease at roughly ₹4,000–₹6,000 per machine per month (**≈₹45,000** for three machines over three months), buy refurbished (**≈₹2L**), or buy new (**₹3.3L**). Buying new is assumed here because the machines outlive the three months and because a slow laptop is the most expensive thing you can give an engineer.
</Callout>

## Contractor alternative

If hiring takes too long, or you want to avoid notice periods and severance inside a three-month window, contractors are the fallback. Rates below are **indicative Indian market ranges** — they vary widely by skill, city and how the work is sourced. **Verify with actual quotes.**

| Option | Indicative day rate | Indicative monthly (20–22 days) | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|--:|--:|---|---|
| Freelance mid-level full-stack | ₹5,000 – ₹9,000 | ₹1,00,000 – ₹1,90,000 | A bounded adapter, a set of screens | Quality variance; needs strong review, which costs SSE time |
| Freelance senior full-stack | ₹9,000 – ₹15,000 | ₹1,80,000 – ₹3,15,000 | Integration work, frontend build-out | Availability; often juggling clients |
| Specialist (AWS/IaC, Postgres, deliverability) | ₹12,000 – ₹25,000 | ₹2,50,000 – ₹5,25,000 | A time-boxed piece: AWS landing zone, migration review, SES warm-up | Best used for days, not months |
| Small agency or studio team | — | ₹1,50,000 – ₹3,50,000 per engineer | Burst capacity with some management included | Knowledge leaves when they do; IP assignment must be explicit |
| Contract designer | ₹8,000 – ₹18,000 | — | 10–15 days of screens: approval queue, onboarding, analytics | Cheapest meaningful quality win in this plan (₹1.5L – ₹3L) |

**How that compares.** An SE at ₹12L CTC costs **₹1,00,000 per month**. An equivalent contractor costs **₹1.5L – ₹3L per month** — roughly **1.5× to 3×**. You pay that premium for: no notice period, no laptop, no recruitment fee, an immediate start and an easy exit. You lose retention, context and anyone who cares about the codebase after December.

<Callout type="warn" title="Contractor costs that are easy to forget">
- **GST at 18%** on invoices from a registered contractor or agency — recoverable as input credit if you are registered, a real 18% cost if you are not.
- **TDS at 10%** under section 194J on professional fees; you must deduct, deposit and file.
- **IP assignment** must be written into the contract. Without it, the code may not be yours.
- **No gratuity, PF or notice** — which is the point, but it also means zero retention leverage.
</Callout>

**Recommendation for this plan:** keep all three core roles as employees. The SSE especially — the architecture, AWS accounts and production knowledge must not walk out on 31 December. Use contractors only for **time-boxed specialist work** (an AWS landing-zone review, SES deliverability setup, or 10–15 days of design) that would otherwise consume SSE capacity.
