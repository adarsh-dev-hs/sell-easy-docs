**Dates:** Tuesday 3 November – Monday 30 November 2026 (weeks 5–8, gate on 30 November).
**Team:** SSE + SE-1 + SE-2. Founder as PM and design-partner owner.

## Objective

> Close the loop on real data. A real intent signal from a real vendor arrives by webhook, the agent scores and drafts, an approver sends, the sequence advances on a schedule from a warmed mailbox, the prospect's reply lands in the inbox, and the resulting deal appears in the partner's HubSpot — for 2–3 design partners, on their own accounts and contacts.

Month 1 made the product real. Month 2 makes it **useful**, and puts it in front of people who did not build it.

<Callout type="info" title="This is the month with the most unknowns">
Three of these epics — the scheduler, reply capture and two-way CRM sync — are the parts of the product that have **never existed**, not mocks being replaced. They are where estimates are least reliable, so they start in week 5 and each has a named reduced version to fall back to.
</Callout>

### E11 · Background job runner — SSE

The deliberately small version of "async". **No EventBridge, no SQS** — those are [post-launch](/docs/roadmap/beyond-the-mvp). Instead: a scheduled worker process on ECS with a Postgres-backed job table (`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`), at-least-once delivery, retry with backoff, a dead-letter state that is visible in the admin UI, and idempotency keys on every job.

Jobs it runs: due sequence steps (every minute), nightly intent re-scoring and decay ([Scoring engine](/docs/engineering/backend/scoring-engine)), sequence-stat rollups, CRM sync retries, and daily mailbox counter resets.

This choice is an explicit trade: it is roughly a week of work versus roughly a month for the event-bus rewrite, and it is sufficient below a few thousand jobs an hour. It is recorded as an ADR with the volume at which it must be revisited.

### E12 · Sequence step scheduler and sending engine — SSE + SE-2

The largest single build of the three months. It makes the already-stored `SequenceSettings` mean something:

- Select `active` enrollments with `nextStepAt ≤ now` that fall inside the configured send window (`sendDays`, `startHour`, `endHour`, `timezone`).
- Pick a mailbox from `mailboxIds` round-robin, respecting `dailyLimit`, and increment `sentToday`.
- Render and send the step through SES, then advance `currentStep` and `nextStepAt` by the next step's `dayOffset`, update `stats.sent`, and log an activity.
- `wait` steps only move `nextStepAt`. Task steps create tasks.
- `stopOnReply` halts the enrollment when a reply is captured; suppression and unsubscribe lists are honoured; the enrollment is marked `completed` after the last step.
- Reset `sentToday` daily.

**Reduced fallback if it slips:** send windows plus daily limits but a single mailbox per sequence, and no time-zone-aware scheduling (workspace time zone only).

### E13 · Intent webhook connector — SE-1

**One vendor, not three. RB2B is the preference; Trigify is the fallback** if RB2B access does not arrive in time. Whichever lands first is the one that ships; the other is deferred.

Signed webhook payloads, vendor-specific mapping to the existing signal shape, replay protection, per-org secrets, and a recorded-fixture test suite so the connector can be finished before partner traffic exists. Once it is live, `POST /signals/simulate` and the Simulate button come out of production ([Tech debt #5](/docs/engineering/tech-debt)).

### E14 · HubSpot two-way sync — SE-1

Contacts, companies and deals. Push from SellEasy on the events that matter (account becomes a target, draft sent, deal stage change), pull on a schedule to catch CRM-side changes, with a documented conflict policy.

**The conflict policy must be decided by 6 November** — CRM wins, SellEasy wins, or most recent wins — because the sync design depends on it ([Decisions](/docs/architecture/decisions#open-decisions)). A private HubSpot app is enough for pilots; **no marketplace listing is attempted**, since the review alone would consume the remaining timeline.

**Reduced fallback:** one-way push from SellEasy to HubSpot plus a scheduled pull of deal stages only.

### E15 · Inbound reply capture — SE-1 + SE-2

IMAP polling or a provider webhook, whichever the partners' mailboxes support. Thread matching back to the enrollment and contact, reply detection driving `stopOnReply`, bounce and out-of-office classification kept simple (bounce, auto-reply, human reply), and messages surfaced in the existing inbox UI. Claude reply suggestions from Month 1 are wired to real threads.

### E16 · Sequence stats and deliverability signals — SE-2

Replace the synthetic per-step performance numbers with real counters from the send path and SES events: sent, delivered, bounced, replied, per step and per enrollment. Mailbox health derived from actual bounce and complaint rates rather than computed in the frontend, with an automatic pause when a mailbox crosses a threshold.

### E17 · Pilot onboarding and partner support — SE-2 + founder

Import the partner's accounts and contacts, configure their ICP and playbooks with them, connect their HubSpot and mailboxes, walk through a first approval together, and set up a weekly scorecard. Every partner gets a named engineer. Feedback goes into one triage list that the founder ranks weekly; only P0 bugs and one-line fixes jump the queue mid-week.

## Where AI assistance helps most this month

| Task | How AI assistance is used | Realistic saving |
|---|---|---|
| HubSpot adapter | Generate the client, object mappings and pagination from the HubSpot API docs plus the existing CRM interface | **High** — this is the single biggest AI win of the plan |
| Intent connector | Generate payload mapping, signature verification and fixture tests from the vendor's sample payloads | High |
| Job runner | Generate the `SKIP LOCKED` queue, retry and backoff code plus its tests | Medium |
| Scheduler | Generate the time-window and time-zone arithmetic **and its property-based tests** — the tests are where the value is, because the edge cases are what break | Medium–high |
| IMAP / reply parsing | Generate thread-matching and header-parsing code with a fixture corpus of real replies | High |
| Partner onboarding material | Generate the per-partner runbook and the weekly scorecard template | High |

It does **not** help with: getting RB2B or Trigify access granted, partner mailbox permissions, the conflict-policy decision, or interpreting what a partner actually means when they say the drafts "feel off".

## Exit criteria (G2 · Pilot gate, 30 November)

- [ ] At least **two design partners** have been live on their own data for **two weeks or more**.
- [ ] For each live partner, the full loop has run at least once end to end: real intent signal → agent run → Claude draft → human approval → email sent from a warmed mailbox → reply captured in the inbox → deal visible in their HubSpot.
- [ ] The scheduler sends **≥ 99% of due steps inside the configured window**, measured over a week.
- [ ] Sequence stats reconcile with SES events; no synthetic numbers remain in the UI.
- [ ] Failed jobs are retried, surface in the dead-letter view, and the dead-letter view is empty at the gate.
- [ ] `POST /signals/simulate` and the Simulate button are gone from production.
- [ ] HubSpot sync survives a conflict scenario per the documented policy, with no duplicate deals created in a week of pilot traffic.
- [ ] Domain and mailbox warm-up has reached the volume the pilots need, with bounce rate ≤ 2%.
- [ ] Every MVP **Must-have** in [MVP scope → In scope](/docs/mvp-scope/in-scope) is either done or has a dated plan inside Month 3.

## Risks specific to Month 2

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scheduler complexity underestimated (time zones, windows, mailbox limits, threading) | **H** | **H** | Start week 5; property-based tests on window arithmetic; named reduced version (single mailbox, workspace time zone) | SSE |
| RB2B **and** Trigify access both delayed | M | **H** | Build against recorded fixtures; the generic webhook plus a Zapier bridge is a working fallback that needs no vendor | SE-1 |
| HubSpot sync takes longer than three weeks | M | **H** | Reduced fallback is one-way push plus deal-stage pull; decide by 20 Nov | SE-1 |
| Reply capture blocked by partner mailbox policy (OAuth scopes, IT approval) | M | M | Ask for mailbox access in the partner kickoff, not in week 7; support both IMAP and provider webhooks | SE-1 |
| Partner feedback creates mid-month scope churn | **H** | M | One ranked triage list, ranked weekly by the founder; only P0 bugs jump the queue | Founder |
| Pilot data reveals real bugs in the existing scoring / dedup / agent logic at real volumes | M | M | It is the point of the pilot — reserve ~20% of Month 3 capacity for burn-down | SSE |
| Deliverability damaged by sending too much too early | M | **H** | Hard daily caps well below warm-up capacity; approval on by default; monitor bounces daily | SSE |

## Cut first if the month is slipping

In this order: **E16** (keep basic counters, drop mailbox health automation) → **E14 reduced** to one-way push plus deal-stage pull → **E15 reduced** to reply detection only, with no threading UI. **E11, E12 and E13 are not cuttable** — without them there is no loop to launch.
